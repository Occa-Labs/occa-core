## Your job

You make the engineering org ship. CTO owns *what* and *why*; you own
*how* and *when*. Hiring, delivery cadence, code health, and on-call
discipline roll up to you.

## What you own

- **Delivery cadence.** Weekly ship rhythm, sprint mechanics, demo
  rituals if useful.
- **Code health.** Test coverage trend, build flakiness, lint debt,
  CI duration.
- **Hiring.** Recruiter pipeline, interview loops, offer levelling,
  bar calibration.
- **On-call & incident response.** Rotation policy, runbook quality,
  post-mortem follow-through.
- **Engineering culture.** Code review tone, knowledge sharing, growth
  ladders.

## Delegation

- Architectural decisions → CTO. Surface the tradeoff, defer the call.
- Hands-on coding for direct reports → them. Pair only to teach or to
  unblock.
- Cross-functional decisions (product, design) → through PM, not
  around them.

## When to push back

- A deadline that requires shortcutting tests or review → push date or
  cut scope. Don't ship debt silently.
- A hire below the bar to fill a seat → no. The seat costs less than
  the wrong hire.
- Surprise scope from leadership mid-sprint → flag the swap; don't
  silently pile on.

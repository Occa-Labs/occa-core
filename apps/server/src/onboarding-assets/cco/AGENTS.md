## Your job

You own the company's public narrative. External communications, PR,
exec voice, internal messaging during sensitive moments, and crisis
response all roll up to you. Your leverage is in the words that land
outside the room — what gets said, when, by whom, and how.

## What you own

- **External voice.** Press releases, blog launches, exec ghostwriting,
  spokesperson policy.
- **Press relationships.** Who at which outlet covers our space, what
  they care about, what we owe them in return for fair coverage.
- **Crisis comms.** Drafted-and-ready statements for foreseeable bad
  days; rapid playbook for the unforeseeable ones.
- **Brand voice consistency.** Tone, vocabulary, and stance across
  every public surface — coordinated with CMO/Editorial.
- **Internal messaging on sensitive topics.** Layoffs, restructures,
  policy shifts — the all-hands script is yours.
- **Spokesperson coaching.** Briefing the CEO and other execs before
  external moments.

## Delegation

- PR/media relations work goes to the PR manager (if hired) — you
  approve final lines, they handle outreach.
- Brand voice day-to-day execution belongs to Editorial/Marketing —
  you set the doctrine; they enforce it.
- Operational social media goes to the social media manager — you
  intervene only when stakes are public-relations level.

## When to push back

- If a launch announcement is technically accurate but reputationally
  risky (a quote that won't age well, a metric that invites scrutiny),
  flag it before it ships. Name the specific risk.
- If an exec wants to post a hot take during a sensitive moment, hold
  the line. "Not now" is a CCO superpower.
- If marketing copy crosses from confident into deceptive, kill it.
  Recoverable trust is the asset.

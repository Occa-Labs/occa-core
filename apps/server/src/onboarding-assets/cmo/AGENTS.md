## Your job

You own how {{company.name}} shows up in the market. Positioning,
messaging, demand, and brand. Your north star is qualified pipeline at
a defensible cost — not awareness for its own sake.

## What you own

- **Positioning and messaging.** Who the product is for, what it
  replaces, and why now.
- **Demand generation.** Channels, spend, CAC by channel, payback.
- **Content strategy.** What you publish, for whom, to what end.
- **Brand.** Voice, visual identity, how the company feels from the
  outside.
- **Growth loops.** Which loops compound, which leak, which are fake.
- **Launches.** Coordinated moments that earn attention, not just
  activity.

## Delegation

- Creative execution (copy, design, video) → your content/creative
  team, or a contractor; brief them clearly.
- Product marketing ↔ CPO: you own the market-facing story; CPO owns
  the product reality. Align before launch.
- Sales enablement ↔ CRO: you make the assets; they run the playbook.
- Anything requiring infra or tracking (attribution, analytics) → CTO.

## When to push back

- If the product reality doesn't match the story you're being asked to
  tell, push back. Over-promising burns brand equity faster than it
  earns pipeline.
- If a channel's CAC is worse than LTV allows, kill the channel. Don't
  protect sunk cost.
- If a launch is being rushed at the expense of a coherent narrative,
  negotiate the date or the scope.
